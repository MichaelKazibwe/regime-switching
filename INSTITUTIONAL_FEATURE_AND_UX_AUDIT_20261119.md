# Feature, module and dashboard audit — 19 November 2026

Finalized **24 November 2026**. Source inventory and references were rechecked; live-screen observations remain dated November 19 and release/test receipts remain dated November 18. This document does not claim a new November 24 runtime, market-data or security qualification.

The app has a substantial research and engineering foundation, but it is not yet a qualified institutional investment platform. Its largest product gap is a connected portfolio decision workflow. Its largest qualification gaps are independent financial data, model evidence, operational resilience and accountable human approval.

The dashboard problem is primarily information architecture. It exposes the research machinery before answering the user's questions. More animation, more metrics or another strategy page would not resolve that problem.

## Why the dashboard feels like a report

| Finding | Observed evidence | Recommended change |
|---|---|---|
| UX1 — the portfolio starts below the page chrome | On the live overview and weekly screens, the header, subnavigation, four market tiles, research disclosure, macro badge, section selector and identity banner occupy most of the first desktop screen. | One compact context header; move the market ribbon to Markets or make it optional. Put the selected portfolio and the next review task first. |
| UX2 — several navigation systems describe the same location | Five sidebar groups, view tabs, a section selector, search and repeated headings coexist. Grouping was added, but the navigation budget remains high. | One primary navigation and one local toolbar. Keep search as a shortcut; remove the redundant section dropdown from the normal desktop journey. |
| UX3 — current context surrounds archived results | The live overview presents a saved portfolio valued on **2025-10-29** alongside market context dated **2026-11-18**. Labels disclose this, but the reader must reconcile the scopes. | Default Home to a clearly selected current proposal or linked paper portfolio. Put archived illustrations in Research/Reports. Display portfolio type, valuation date and currency together. This is a context problem, not evidence that the saved calculations are wrong. |
| UX4 — risk has multiple unrelated meanings on one page | The risk view combines archived plan preferences, fixed-weight diagnostics, model outcomes, operational health and forward-observation material. | Separate portfolio risk, model evidence and system operations. Operators get a dedicated operational view; normal users see only relevant portfolio issues. |
| UX5 — explanations are embedded in primary tables | Weekly holdings have six columns including long per-asset rationale; projections and method paragraphs follow as a long document. | Default to asset, target weight, change and contribution. Open rationale, assumptions and provenance in an accessible detail drawer. Keep complete tables in analyst mode and the PDF. |
| UX6 — technically precise labels lack an immediate user question | Model volatility, effective holdings, signed variance shares, probability scores and revision hashes compete with the action the user needs to take. | Lead with plain labels and units; retain the technical definition on demand. Do not invent a qualitative risk rating or confidence percentage without a defined, validated policy. |
| UX7 — the app does not lead with changes or exceptions | There is no single home view showing allocation changes, why they occurred, what requires review and what is unchanged since the last comparable version. | Add a semantic portfolio change log and review inbox. Compare weights and economic inputs, not just changing artifact IDs. A new daily context stamp does not necessarily mean a new weekly allocation. |
| UX8 — decorative whitespace and long forms both consume attention | Large rounded panels, tall status rows and repeated metadata precede useful content; detailed weekly tables require horizontal scrolling. CSS specifies a 1,000px minimum table width. | Use compact context, three or four essential summaries, one dominant analytical visual and a small review queue. Allow an optional dense analyst workspace. Retain readable type and accessible table alternatives. |
| UX9 — older fixed-count language survives | The live overview still has “Explore 28 assets” while its selected research set reports 32. The template contains legacy count labels. | Derive every count from the same selected-universe contract. Add content-consistency tests for navigation, empty states and documentation. |

These findings do not mean deleting disclosures. Keep research status, missing/stale data, portfolio identity, blocked actions and uncertainty visible at the point where they matter. Move methodology, hashes and long explanations to evidence views. This follows the [progressive-disclosure principle](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/): expose essentials first and make advanced detail available on request.

## Recommended dashboard structure

The primary questions should be: **What am I looking at? What changed? Where is the risk? What requires my review?**

| Destination | Default content | Secondary detail |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Selected portfolio/proposal, compact dates/currency/status, key changes, portfolio-versus-benchmark history when a comparable verified series exists, review queue | Data coverage and source details |
| Portfolios | Holdings, asset weights, cash, capital versus risk allocation, verified underlying exposures, proposed changes | Lot/cost/FX details where qualified; holdings evidence |
| Analysis | Risk, historical performance, cycle stress and macro context using the same selected portfolio and period | Quantitative settings, model diagnostics and scenario methodology |
| Research & reports | Weekly candidates, dynamic strategy versions, experiments, archived illustrations, reports and exports | Full statistical evidence, protocol/source identities and audit records |
| Administration — authorized operators only | Jobs, failures, freshness, security/recovery evidence and access administration | Logs, traces, incident and recovery procedures |

Markets/watchlists can be a secondary destination or configurable panel. A broker account balance, actual investment return or live P&L must not be invented when no such account is connected. Show proposal capital as an illustration and paper returns as paper returns. A projection is never the main historical performance line.

Recommended interactions: selecting a holding highlights its risk contribution and exposures; selecting a period updates chart, cards and comparison together; opening a proposed change shows prior/new weight, reason, assumptions and qualified cost estimate; selecting an alert opens the exact affected portfolio/version. Unknown costs or exposure coverage remain unknown, rather than becoming zero.

The accompanying concept illustrates information hierarchy only. It uses the dated November 18 research snapshot, makes no live-account claim and is not a deployed redesign.

## New product services worth adding

These are proposed integration boundaries, not claims that these files already exist:

1. **Portfolio context/view-model service:** one typed source for portfolio type, date, currency, metric/benchmark basis and review state across all views.
2. **Portfolio change and decision-summary service:** deterministic differences between comparable revisions, concise data-grounded reasons and links back to existing evidence. An LLM is not required; if used, it must not invent market facts or recommendations.
3. **Review inbox and proposal lifecycle:** assignments, status, revision comparisons and exact-version decisions using the existing immutable review foundation.
4. **Data catalogue and quality-exception workflow:** rights, provider/observation/availability dates, coverage, revisions, reconciliation and approved use per dataset.
5. **Model inventory and validation workflow:** consolidate existing source/protocol/forecast evidence with accountable independent review and monitoring.
6. **Connected performance/exposure reporting:** consume accounting and look-through services to explain portfolio results and risks rather than adding another disconnected table.

## What “institutional grade” should mean here

First qualify an **institutional research and decision-support workbench**. Personalized advisory and live-account operation are further service tiers, with additional evidence and jurisdiction/business-model review. A prettier page, more assets or an AI assistant does not qualify any tier by itself.

For the research tier, the acceptance bundle should cover reproducibility, licensed/qualified data, economic accounting, independent model review, clear human decision authority, monitored service behavior, security, recoverability and comprehensible reporting. The [BCBS risk-data principles](https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs239.htm) are a useful benchmark for accurate, complete and timely risk reporting; they are not asserted to be automatically binding on this app. The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) provides a voluntary governance reference, not an institutional certification.

Free public macro data, open-source libraries and the existing VM can support further pilot work. They do not guarantee free, licensed global securities histories, historical fund holdings, independent validation or a contractual institutional service level. Keep actual coverage and rights gaps explicit.
